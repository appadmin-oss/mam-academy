Turn 4 · The academic machinery

Grading, curriculum, calendar

Three staff surfaces that decide whether a mark, a level and a refund can be defended a year later. These are power tools for instructors and the registrar, so they carry more per screen than the student side does — but they stay on a phone, because a moderation panel meets in a room and a term gets closed from wherever the registrar is.

A mark becomes defensible

Agreement between markers is measured and shown, the pass mark is set by a panel rather than chosen, and every change after ratification is an entry with a name against it.

A level stops drifting

Curriculum is versioned by curriculum year and students are pinned to the one they entered under, so changing a course next term cannot move the goalposts for someone halfway through it.

A refund has an author

Term dates become data — add/drop, withdrawal, census — and the refund percentage is computed from a published table, adjudicated in Lagos time, identical for two families withdrawing on the same day.

Turn 2 · The learning engine

A portal that trains, not one that reports

Turn 1 fixed navigation. This turn adds the part that actually grows a student: a deliberate-practice loop with a target and a recorded take, the teach-back that the research supports, spaced retrieval, an honest mastery report, and a named human in the loop. Everything here is drawn from your own research documents — including the places where they say don't.

2a What I added, and the evidence behind each call

Six additions

1 · Practice room — a deliberate-practice loop, not a minute counter

Today's practice logs minutes, which measures compliance rather than improvement. The new loop is target → attempt → recorded take → self-mark the breakdown point → instructor sees it. Your repo already ships the hard parts: practice-player.js has waveform, tempo scaling, A–B loop and timestamped markers, and take-recorder.js records. They are currently buried inside panels; this makes them the screen.

2 · Teach it back — three dimensions, not six

The teach-back spine is the best-supported thing in your model — the protégé effect, self-explanation, preparing-to-teach beating preparing-to-be-tested. Your own review flags the scoring, not the method: six subjective dimensions, one untrained rater, overlapping constructs. So the student-facing flow collapses to three self-checks and the score is explicitly formative, with a visible "your instructor will listen to this" rather than a number that carries stakes.

3 · Keep it warm — spaced retrieval, opt-in

Three questions a day drawn from weeks already covered, self-rated, rescheduled by that rating. Spacing and retrieval practice are the two most robust findings in the cognitive-science set, and this is the cheapest way to operationalise them. It measures a behaviour the student opts into, which is the line your research draws between practice tracking and surveillance.

4 · Mastery, reported honestly

A band, not a decimal. Coverage stated on the face ("8 of 11 components evidenced · 72% of the weight"). "Not enough evidence yet" as a visible row, never a silent zero and never renormalised away. Horizontal bars per competency, no radar — radar scores worst for accuracy and misleads through area. And the 80% threshold is labelled as a convention rather than a finding, because it is one.

5 · Your next step — supportive, plus a named human

Intervention flags appear as a forward-looking action ("let's spend this week on rhythm"), never as a status the student wears. The labelling literature is the reason: an explicit at-risk label increases social distance and can become self-fulfilling, so the flag stays staff-side and only the next step surfaces. Beside it sits the single most consistent feature across SNHU, WGU and UoPeople — an assigned, named person reachable in one tap. Automation without that hurts retention.

6 · Lesson credits — a real ledger

Make-ups are currently ad-hoc rescheduling. The music-school standard is an explicit credit ledger: 24-hour notice, a yearly cap, non-transferable credits that expire, no cash refund in lieu, and a teacher cancellation always yielding a credit. Stating the rule on the screen where the student spends the credit is what makes it feel fair rather than arbitrary.

Three things I deliberately did not build

The cohort effort board

It exists in the student portal today. Gamification's measured effect on intrinsic motivation is small (g ≈ 0.26), extrinsic-reward-heavy designs can reduce it, and for minors the Children's Code forbids manipulative nudges. A leaderboard among ~20 named children is the highest-risk form. The streak stays — it's a personal commitment device, not a ranking — and effort recognition moves into the private "you're on your best run this term" framing.

Any at-risk indicator visible to the student or guardian

Staff-side only. What the family sees is the booking, not the flag.

Automatic audio scoring

Recording and hosting takes for human rubric marking is the right scope. Reliable pitch/rhythm assessment is not realistic here, and a wrong automatic mark on a child's playing costs more trust than the feature is worth.

Two structural changes

  • The bottom bar becomes Home · Train · Learn · Attend · More. Pay leaves the bar and appears on Home whenever something is owed — for an under-16 student the guardian pays anyway, and Train earns the slot because it is the only destination the student is meant to open daily.
  • Every write says what happened. "Saved on this phone — will upload when you're back online" rather than an optimistic tick. On metered, intermittent Lagos data a dishonest success state loses a practice log, and a lost log breaks the streak the whole loop depends on.

Mobile-first portals

Built on the repo's own light portal tokens (portal.css — ink #1B2430, restrained gold #E2650F, semantic status hues). Same palette, new structure: the 22-link drawer becomes a five-slot bottom bar, and every screen opens on the next action rather than six KPI tiles.

1a UX audit — current portal on mobile

Summary

The flow is sound; the surface is the problem. Three defects account for most of the confusion: primary navigation is a 22-link drawer hidden behind a Menu button, the Overview opens with six stat tiles instead of the one thing the student must do today, and the student's coursework is spread across four differently-named tabs (My Learning, My Courses, Classes, Curriculum) — a leak of the two parallel enrolment models into the user's mental model.

Assessed from source (portal/*/index.html, assets/css/portal.css). Contrast values and focus order below are read from the CSS, not measured in a browser; timing and screen-reader output would need a live check.

Findings

SEV 4All 22 destinations are hidden behind one Menu button

Where: .portal-side becomes a transform:translateX(-100%) drawer under 880px; the only affordance is .portal-side-toggle ("Menu").

Why it matters: every navigation on a phone costs open → scan five group headings → tap, and the current location is only visible inside the drawer. Hiding navigation is a measured discoverability loss; at 22 items with no search it also exceeds what anyone scans without reading.

Fix: four primary destinations plus More in a persistent bottom bar; keep the long list inside More, grouped as today.

SEV 4Overview spends the first screen on statistics, not on today

Where: renderOverview() — greeting, then six kpi() tiles (streak, this week, active learning, assignments, attendance, applications), then progress, then panels.

Why it matters: .kpi-row is minmax(200px,1fr), so on a 390px viewport the six tiles stack into roughly 600px of vertical space. Checking in, paying tuition, and seeing the next class — the three reasons a student opens this on a phone — are all below the fold, and check-in is a different tab entirely.

Fix: lead with one time-bound action card and a short "needs you" list; demote stats to a single three-across strip.

SEV 4Four tabs are all "my coursework"

Where: My Learning, My Courses, Classes and Curriculum in the Learn group; Grades and My Progress in Progress.

Why it matters: the split follows the legacy classes model versus the cohort/course model, which is an internal distinction. A student cannot predict which tab holds their timetable, and the same enrolment can appear in two of them. This is the single largest contributor to "it's just confusing".

Fix: one Learn destination — courses with progress, weeks, certificates, class timetable — the dual-read the code already does, presented as one thing. Curriculum becomes a section inside a course.

SEV 3Status pills print raw database vocabulary

Where: .pill is text-transform:lowercase and payments render p.status verbatim — "succeeded", and in attendance "left_early", "technical_failure".

Fix: a label map at the render boundary. "Paid", "Left early", "Class cancelled". Check-in already has statusLabels — apply it everywhere.

SEV 3Display & access sits at the foot of a hidden drawer

Why it matters: text size, dyslexia-friendly type and reduced-data mode are real and already persisted pre-paint — but the users who need them most reach them last, after opening a drawer and scrolling past 22 links. Same for the theme toggle.

Fix: promote it to the top of the More sheet and into Profile, with the current state visible rather than hidden in a popover.

SEV 3A seven-column payment table becomes seven rows per payment

Where: Payment History — Reference, Purpose, Provider, Amount, Status, Verified, Receipt. The mobile card pattern is good, but it gives a word-break:break-all reference and "Provider" the same weight as the amount.

Fix: amount, date and state on the card face; reference and provider on the receipt behind it.

SEV 3Emoji carries state that nothing else carries

"All settled 🎉", "All done 🎉", "On fire 🔥" appear inside KPI sub-labels. A screen reader announces the glyph name, and in reduced-data mode the tone is all that is left of the signal. Say the state in words and keep the glyph optional.

SEV 3The under-16 variant changes type size, not structure

body[data-variant="ktams"] warms the gold, enlarges nav links and adds a practice hero — then hands a twelve-year-old the same 22-link drawer and the same seven-column tables. The fix is fewer destinations for everyone, which the bottom bar delivers, plus a larger default target (48px) rather than a variant-only bump.

SEV 2"New Application" is the header CTA on every screen

Including for a student already enrolled in a class. On a phone it takes the header's remaining width from the page title. Make the header action contextual, or drop it into More.

SEV 2The quick-add FAB has no bar to live on

.cmdk-fab floats at bottom:18px;right:18px over content whose container reserves 64px. It overlaps the last row of any list, and a command palette is a keyboard idea doing navigation's job on a touch device.

SEV 1Sidebar group headings are the smallest text on the screen

0.62rem uppercase at --text-muted — they carry all the wayfinding in a 22-item list and read as decoration.

What's working — keep it

  • The light theme is a genuinely careful token system, with measured contrast ratios in the comments and separate text-safe siblings for accent colours (--gold-text, --warn-text). The redesign uses it unchanged.
  • Table-to-card at 640px with data-label pseudo-elements, rather than horizontal scroll.
  • Skeleton loading instead of a spinner; tabular-nums on every column of figures.
  • Accessibility preferences applied pre-paint from localStorage, and prefers-reduced-motion honoured throughout.
  • 44px input floor with 16px font size to defeat iOS focus zoom; dvh and safe-area insets already in the drawer.

What I'd verify before acting

  • Which four destinations earn the bottom bar. A first-click test over the 22 labels settles it in an afternoon and is far cheaper than shipping a guess — my proposal below is Home, Learn, Attend, Pay.
  • Whether students currently find Payments at all. Tab-open counts from the SPA would answer it directly.
  • Focus order and screen-reader output through the drawer and the a11y popover — readable from the code, but it needs a real device pass with the under-16 cohort and at least one assistive-tech user.
